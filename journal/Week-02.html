How does the seasonal rainful at Paris related to its monthly temperature?

What I found

The temperature decreases from the equator to the poles. The temperature is high and the pressure is low in the equatorial region, causing hot air to rise and forming a circulation with the subtropical high pressure. The data from 1981 to 2020 shows that the highest average monthly temperature in Paris occurs in August, and the highest average monthly rainfall occurs in May. The overall temperature is almost no more than 20 degrees Celsius, and the rainfall is relatively average throughout the year. Therefore, according to data, Paris has cool summers and mild winters, with winters being drier than summers. The overall climate is a Marine west coast climate. Paris fits into the larger climate pattern of Western Europe. And thanks to global westerly winds and the warm North‑Atlantic ocean current, this whole area gets mild winters and rainfall spread out pretty evenly over all twelve months. Since Paris sits a little ways inland in the Basin, away from the ocean, its temperatures swing a bit more between summer and winter, and it gets less total rain than coastal places such as Brest. On a global scale, its mild winters stand out. Other spots at about the same latitude in Canada or Russia have much colder winters, because they do not get that warming push from the North‑Atlantic Current.

What this does not show

This map layer mixes temperature and precipitation together in its color coding. It does not show temperature and rainfall separately. So I cannot use this map to answer my original question: how seasonal rainfall in Paris relates to its monthly temperature. It's difficult for me to tell if warmer months bring more or less rain, because the single color for each station combines both climate variables.
