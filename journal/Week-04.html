Green

The question

To what extent does NDVI green‑pattern across three major Paris urban parks correspond to actual on‑site ecological density of habitat?

The data

Sensor: Landsat 8, Landsat 9 spatial resolution: 30 m per pixel Scene acquired 10/03/2026 cloud cover 18% (additional reference scene: 09/25/2026) The vegetation index used is NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index), calculated from Band 4 (red) and Band 5 (near‑infrared). Data source: Esri Multispectral Landsat image service from ArcGIS Living Atlas(this service operates under an open token‑free licence supporting anonymous web access.)

The method

In ArcGIS Online Map Viewer I applied the NDVI Colorized processing template for visual rendering. In Landsat Explorer I set an index mask threshold range 0.5‑1.0. This threshold was chosen on intuitive grounds: values above 0.5 were treated as “green vegetation”, with no pre‑existing scientific justification for this cut‑off value. I sampled NDVI pixel values for three named park sites: Bois de Vincennes, Parc des Buttes‑Chaumont, and Parc de Sceaux. I also sampled a nearby sports‑stadium artificial grass pitch as a high‑index test location.

What I found

My high‑index, low‑habitat location is the large irrigated sports‑stadium grass pitch beside Autoroute de l’Est, with sampled NDVI value 0.555. Although the index registers strong greenness, this manicured sports turf supports almost no native wildlife habitat. Among my three pre‑selected parks: Bois de Vincennes scores NDVI 0.698; Parc des Buttes‑Chaumont scores NDVI 0.650; Parc de Sceaux scores NDVI 0.564.

What this does not show

I am using NDVI values to represent how good an area is as wildlife habitat, and this proxy can be wrong because well‑watered, closely‑cut sports grass can get fairly high NDVI numbers, much like park greenery. Yet this sports grass gives almost nowhere for local wild species to live. This NDVI reading only shows light reflected off leaves on 25 September 2026 and October 3rd 2026. It cannot tell the difference between heavily maintained grass and more diverse park woodland. It also tells us nothing about the plants and animals living under the tree canopy, what species are present, or how well habitats connect to each other.
