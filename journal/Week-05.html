Water

The question

Where does the runoff from my Paris site flow, and where does the drinking water supply of this site originate? Which actors hold claims over these water resources?

The data

Watershed catchment layer: Global Watersheds tool, MERIT‑Hydro (2021) and HydroSHEDS (2013), low/high precision modes, open-source, no license restriction. Aqueduct 4.0 Water Risk layer: World Resources Institute (WRI), CC BY 4.0 license, baseline period 1979–2019, data vintage April 2023. The water stress value is calculated at the HydroBASINS Level 6 sub-basin scale. The value displayed on the map is fixed for the full hydrological sub-basin, even when the polygon has been split by French administrative boundaries.

I am using the delineated catchment boundary at my site to stand for where my site’s water actually goes and comes from, and it might fail because the routine has no representation of pipes, so it describes rain on the roof and not the tap water supply. I am using baseline water stress for my basin to stand for whether water at my site is adequate, and it might fail because it counts total available renewable volume, so water that is present but polluted and unusable still counts as supply. I am using X to stand for Y, and it might fail because Z.

The method

I delineated the catchment using MERIT‑Hydro and HydroSHEDS datasets in Global Watersheds website, switching between low and high precision settings. I recorded the pour point coordinate and the snap distance between my input site coordinate and the algorithm-selected pour point.The water stress statistic is computed for the full HydroBASINS Level 6 sub-basin. The visible polygon on the ArcGIS map is this hydrological basin clipped by national/administrative borders. I calculated the area ratio between this clipped polygon and my approximate site area (0.1 km²). I also compared annual baseline water stress values with monthly values to observe seasonal variability.

What I found

The engineered drinking water pipeline network serving Paris crosses the hydrological divide: it transports raw water from reservoirs and intake points outside the surface catchment to the urban site, flowing toward the city for municipal supply.My three delineations from different precision/dataset settings produced highly consistent catchment areas around 44000 sqkm. This consistency matches expectations from the local geography: the Seine basin has clear, stable topographic divides, so different elevation datasets agree on the watershed boundary. My initial hand estimate of 10000 km² drastically underestimated the real basin scale.

What this does not show

Between the two proxies, the water stress index carries greater risk.The catchment boundary is precise for surface rainfall runoff: it reliably answers the narrow question “where does rooftop rainwater flow?” Its limits are clear and confined to drinking-water supply. The water stress index, by contrast, invites readers to make broad claims about local water availability for residents, even though the statistic is averaged over an enormous sub-basin, ignores water quality, and hides large monthly seasonal variation behind a single annual summary value. This mismatch between what the map visually appears to claim and what the data actually measures is more likely to mislead interpretation.
