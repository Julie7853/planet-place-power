Terrain and Water

The question

Where in my Paris site do terrain pattern, scale and slope density affect surface water flow?

The data

The method

The first part is adding layer by searching in "living Altas". Layer of "terrain", "terrain-slope degrees", "terrain-slopemap", "World elevation coverage map", terrain -dark multi-direcional hilshade" and "world soils 250m soil organic carbon" were added in order on the map. Next step is reading data via the layers. To see every detailed information clearly and not get confused with different layers, I turned on layers one by one, by clicking the "eye" button. For example, leaving the "Terrain-slope degrees" only, you can see the map covered with black-gray-white color, showing the slope degrees from high to low through the color of light to dark. Click anywhere on your map, you can get mumeric data for that very region. Fianlly, I tried to change some parameters to adjust the look of the layers. An important parameter is the "transparency", I changed it to around 23% so that I can see the adminastrative boundaries of my site and the terrain at the same time. Especailly, for the "terrain slope degrees", opening Styles on the Settings toolbar, I narrowed the range so that the full colour ramp is squeezed into the slopes that actually occur at my site. Then, I selected "Classify -> Style options -> Manual breaks -> set Number of classes", and set 6 breaks for the slope, and then raise the maximum slope from the origin 16 to 27, set the breaks at 0,1,4,8 and 16.( The breaks of 0,1,4,and 8 are the original breaks so I keep it. And 16 is the origianl limit, as the difference of 16 to 27 is 11, the value is appropriate so I set it as a new break.) At last, all the layers are ready, I can adjust any parameters to meet my research oor observation goals.

What I found

At this Paris site, the ground slopes gently from west toward the east and the Seine River, so rainwater flows eastward toward the Seine during rainfall. Eroded surface material moves down the gentle slope with surface runoff toward the river channel. Most transported materials will eventually settle and deposit on the Seine’s floodplain.

What this does not show

For example, this map and layers can not support the claim that the slope difference between the left and right bank of Seine River (or the maximum difference in slope of the city Paris) is smaller than 3 degrees (27 degrees). Because the data uses a 1m cell size, but slope values are averaged across each grid cell. Tiny, short, steep river banks are smaller than the grid’s averaging window. The map smooths these small sharp slopes and cannot prove this claim. Also the vertical uncertainty is 0.20m, which is enough to create elevation error.
